# /qml-screen — essence

## What this skill is for
Killing ideas on paper, in hours, before they cost compute — and when an idea survives, saying
exactly what shape it survives in. Historically the screen questions (readout, resource cap,
twin) killed more of our ideas, more cheaply, than any experiment did.

## The judgment calls it makes
- **Does this brush a known dead end — by mechanism?** The exclusion ledger is engaged, not looked
  up: same mechanism, or a changed precondition?
- **Which rule decides it?** Readout shape and cadence, the resource cap charged to both arms, the
  classical twin, then real data, the operating point and the frontier. At least one must be
  decisive, or the hypothesis is not yet sharp enough.
- **Where could the quantum step sit?** Enumerate deployment architectures; one may survive where
  the others die (Q-FEAT-screen died, Q-FEAT-core survived).
- **Is this a question for literature?** Then ask deep research a scoped question and resume —
  do not guess.

## What success looks like
PASS with the constraints the pre-registration must honor; PASS-NARROWED with the smaller claim
that survives; or KILLED-ON-PAPER with a named mechanism and the narrowest class it generalizes to
— which becomes a ledger entry that protects everyone after us.

## The failure it guards against
"Promising, needs an experiment" on every axis. And its opposite: killing a whole field because
one construction failed.
